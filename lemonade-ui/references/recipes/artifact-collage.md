# Recipe: Artifact Collage

Use when the product is best proven through several fragments: records, cards, files, messages, clips, screenshots, logs, invoices, orders, notes, or outputs.

## Structure

- Pick one dominant artifact.
- Add two to four supporting fragments with different scale, density, or depth.
- Arrange fragments around a clear story, not as random cards.
- Use overlap, crop, rotation, or z-depth to create a composed scene.

## Layout Moves

- Dominant selected object with small before/after fragments nearby.
- Central product surface with floating proof chips and annotation arrows.
- Stacked files/cards showing input, active state, and output.
- Timeline fragment plus selected detail card.
- Media/editor crop plus export/result card.

## Type

- Headline can sit above, beside, or inside the collage.
- Keep labels tiny but readable.
- Use product microcopy to make fragments credible.
- Avoid each card needing a paragraph.

## Material

- Mix surface types deliberately: one solid panel, one translucent overlay, one small chip, one highlighted selected state.
- Use shadows and borders to clarify stack order.
- Keep accents tied to state: warning, complete, selected, due, live, ready.

## Mobile

- Do not stack every fragment.
- Show the dominant artifact plus one supporting fragment.
- Hide or simplify decorative annotations.
- Let the user understand the product from one object.

## Avoid

- Equal-size card soup.
- Decorative cards with generic labels.
- Too many fragments competing for first read.
- Collage that hides the actual product job.
