# Composition Discipline

Use this when a design starts feeling like a noisy poster, Dribbble collage, fake concept shot, or overcorrection away from dashboards.

Lemonade UI can be bold, cinematic, and artifact-led. It still needs restraint, legibility, and product plausibility.

## The Discipline Rule

Every first viewport needs:

- One primary read.
- One dominant artifact.
- One accent system.
- One spatial idea.
- One clear action.

If there are two huge reads, several accent colors, random rotations, multiple floating cards, and a giant shape behind everything, the page is not premium. It is noise.

## Artifact Poster Rules

Standalone product artifacts should feel like useful objects, not fake portfolio props.

- Keep important artifact text horizontal.
- Use rotation only when it clarifies depth; default max is 1-2 degrees.
- Do not rotate the main artifact and then add another rotated backing shape.
- Do not let the artifact cover, collide with, or visually crush the headline.
- Do not use a giant decorative shape behind the artifact unless the shape is the page's main frame.
- Make object labels plausible: names, dates, actions, statuses, outputs. Avoid fake scoring theatre.
- Leave enough quiet space around the artifact for the eye to understand it.

## Headline Discipline

- A hero headline should not become one-word-per-line unless the user asked for brutal poster typography.
- If the headline sits beside a large artifact, keep it to 2-3 compact lines.
- If the headline is huge, the supporting copy and CTAs must be quieter and closer.
- Do not put a giant headline inside a boxed panel unless the panel is intentionally the hero frame.
- Rewrite before shrinking when the line breaks are awkward.

## Palette Discipline

- Use one main accent and at most one support accent.
- Avoid acid/neon colors on multiple unrelated objects.
- Do not mix loud lime, cyan, cream, orange, and black unless the brand demands that range.
- Accents should map to state: selected, ready, warning, complete, live, due.
- If the product artifact is already bright, keep the background quieter.

## Nav And Chrome

- Navigation should not become the hero object unless the product is navigation.
- Avoid oversized floating nav bars that compete with the actual product proof.
- Brand fallback should feel deliberate, but small. Do not make an initial badge plus category text the loudest UI after the headline.

## Poster-Collage Rejects

Reject or revise when:

- The main artifact is tilted enough to make the UI feel unserious.
- A giant headline, giant card, giant accent shape, and floating note all compete.
- The artifact is a fake business card with random labels instead of a real product state.
- The page uses decorative confidence scores, fake savings badges, or novelty labels as proof.
- CTA and nav feel like afterthoughts floating around the composition.
- The screenshot would fail if motion, glow, and rotation were removed.

## Repair Moves

- Straighten the main artifact.
- Remove one floating fragment.
- Use one accent color across CTA, selected state, and one product chip.
- Reduce headline size and improve line breaks.
- Replace fake metric badges with an actual output, receipt, transcript, note, or next action.
- Align the artifact and copy on a shared axis.
- Convert a poster card into a more believable document, receipt, message, file, object, or product output.
