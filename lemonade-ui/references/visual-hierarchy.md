# Visual Hierarchy

Use this for layout, typography, spacing, material, first-viewport composition, and "why does this look worse even though it has nice pieces?"

## Static-First

The static screenshot must look good before hover, scroll, or animation. If motion is the only interesting part, the design is weak.

## First Read

The user should understand, in order:

1. What this is.
2. Why it matters.
3. What proof or product artifact supports it.
4. What action to take.

Do not let headline, CTA, stats, mockup, nav, and badges all compete at the same intensity.

## Type Rules

- Use giant type only when the surrounding layout has room to breathe.
- On mobile, reduce headline ambition before compressing everything else.
- Keep hero-scale type out of panels, dashboards, cards, and controls.
- Avoid viewport-width font sizing.
- Avoid negative letter spacing.
- Headline should be shorter than the supporting copy.
- If the headline wraps into too many lines, rewrite it before shrinking it.

## Spacing Rules

- Use whitespace to group and rank content.
- Use density only when the product category needs it, such as dashboards or admin tools.
- Do not put large cards beside large cards unless the contrast in purpose is obvious.
- Avoid equal padding everywhere; important surfaces need different rhythm.
- Reserve space for hover/selected/loading states.

## Material And Color

- Use surface, border, shadow, and accent to clarify hierarchy.
- Avoid making every panel frosted, rounded, and softly shadowed.
- Use one or two accents. Let neutrals do most of the work.
- If everything is outlined, nothing is important.
- If everything glows, nothing is premium.

## Composition Fixes

- Too much text: compress copy and let product evidence move up.
- Too boxed-in: remove one container or let a section become full-bleed.
- Too flat: increase contrast between primary artifact and support content.
- Too busy: remove labels, badges, borders, or secondary CTAs.
- Too generic: add domain-specific states, media, or workflow details.
